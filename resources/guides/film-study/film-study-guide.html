Why Film Matters

Improve Without Taxing Your Body.

Film study is one of the best ways to gain skill without taxing your body. You only get so many hours on the court before fatigue or injury sets in, but with film, you can improve your game while sitting still.

The hardest part is watching objectively. Most players only see the highlights or beat themselves up over mistakes. Learn to see the reality of what happened without ego and you will grow faster.

Objective

Separate what actually happened from how the result made you feel.

Detailed

Look beyond points and highlights at habits that directly affect winning.

Actionable

Finish with a clear plan you can carry into the next practice.

Watch film like a pro. Find the details that impact winning.
Process mindset: This worksheet is not a highlight review or a punishment. It is a repeatable way to identify your next one-percent improvement.

Set The Session

Watch after your emotions from the game have settled.
Keep a pen nearby and answer based on the film, not your memory.
Review the entire game, including possessions where you did not touch the ball.
Look for habits you can repeat or correct, not proof that you played well or poorly.
Your standard: The purpose is accuracy. Do not protect your ego and do not attack yourself.

Impact On Winning

Objective Questions

Grade The Habits That Win Games.

Did I miss any box outs?    Yes / No
Did I miss any play calls?    Yes / No
Did I miss any help-side rotations on defense?    Yes / No
Did my player blow past me one-on-one?    Yes / No
Did I fail to use my fouls wisely when I had fouls to give?    Yes / No
This is not about stats. It is about habits that impact winning.
Follow the possession: When the answer is yes, write down the time and watch what created the mistake before it happened.
