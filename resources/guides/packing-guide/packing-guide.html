The Packing Rule

Pack For Your Job.
Buy For Your Life.

When you go overseas, your body becomes your business. The right gear helps you stay healthy, study film, recover, and feel settled. But you do not need to move your whole house across the world.

Protect what is hard to replace. Buy the ordinary stuff there.
Pack From Home

Specialized training tools, prescription items, preferred shoes, key electronics, and personal comfort items.

Carry On

Everything you need to survive the first 48 hours and anything you cannot afford to lose.

Buy There

Bulky, inexpensive household items such as kitchen tools, cleaning supplies, toys, and basic bedding.

For families: You will buy toys, small appliances, storage bins, and everyday items that you may later give away or throw out. That is normal. You are living, not camping. Put an arrival budget aside for making the new place feel like home.
Do not leave important gear to chance: You can ship packages from the United States, but I would not rely on international shipping for anything expensive, difficult to replace, or essential to your season. Packages can be delayed, lost, stolen, or charged additional customs fees. Bring those items when you first travel or have someone you trust carry them on a later trip.

Product examples are practical references, not endorsements or guarantees. Airline baggage rules, electrical standards, customs rules, and medical requirements vary. Confirm current requirements before travel.

Overseas Packing Guide04
Control Center

Electronics

Stay Connected.
Stay Prepared.

1

Work + Film

Laptop or tablet: film study, contracts, banking, email, and calls.
Headphones: flights, focus, recovery, and pregame routine.
HDMI cable + dongles: put film on the apartment or hotel TV.
Portable drive or cloud backup: keep passport scans, contracts, medical records, and film accessible.
2

Power + Connection

Universal plug adapters: one in your carry-on and one in your checked bag.
Voltage check: confirm every device supports the country's voltage before plugging it in. An adapter does not convert voltage.
Power bank: keep your phone alive during arrival, travel, and long bus days.
Unlocked phone: makes a local SIM or eSIM much easier.
Extra charging cables: small item, huge annoyance when one breaks.
Do not check: laptops, tablets, cameras, hard drives, prescription devices, or spare lithium batteries. Confirm your airline's current battery rules.

Life Off The Court

Clothes + Comfort

Bring Enough.
Leave Room To Live.

Clothing

Climate-appropriate layers
Practice gear for several days
Two professional outfits
Comfortable travel clothes
Slides, walking shoes, rain gear

Comfort

Favorite hoodie or small blanket
Pillowcase or compact pillow
Spices or shelf-stable snacks
Journal, devotional, books
A few photos from home
Comfort is not softness. Feeling settled helps you perform.
3

Do Not Overpack

Do not pack a full season of toiletries or cleaning supplies.
Do not fill bags with cheap kitchen appliances that may not match local voltage.
Do not bring clothes you never wear at home.
Leave space for team gear, gifts, and purchases you will bring back.
Overseas Packing Guide06
Family Move

Spouse + Kids

Build A Home,
Not A Campsite.

4

Bring

Passports, visas, marriage certificate, birth certificates, and several secure digital copies.
Medical records, prescriptions, vaccination records, and school documents.
A small set of favorite toys, books, comfort items, and familiar snacks.
Child car-seat plan confirmed before landing.
Essential baby supplies for the first several days, plus brand photos for replacements.
5

Buy After Arrival

Toys that are bulky, inexpensive, and easy to donate before the next move.
Kitchen appliances built for local plugs and voltage.
Laundry baskets, storage bins, cleaning products, basic dishes, and extra bedding.
Seasonal items after you understand the real climate and apartment.
Budget honestly: Some things will not come home with you. That does not make them wasted. If an item makes daily family life calmer for nine months, it did its job.
