The Recovery Hierarchy

No Gadget Can Replace The Basics.

The best recovery tools are still the simplest ones: whole foods, quality sleep, hydration, and a training plan your body can recover from. If those are missing, no supplement or machine will save you.

I still believe in looking for a one-percent edge. Over a long season, small advantages can add up. The key is to use tools as support for a strong routine, not as a substitute for one.

1. Foundation

Sleep, food, hydration, movement, and an honest workload.

2. Target

Choose a tool for a specific need, not because it looks professional.

3. Repeat

The useful routine is the one you can follow through travel and games.

Master the ninety-nine percent. Then earn the one-percent edge.
My rule: Start with the smallest stack that solves the problem. Add only what you can explain, afford, travel with, and use consistently.

Decision Ladder

Match The Tool To The Problem

Do Not Recover On Autopilot.

1

Name The Need

General fatigue or poor sleep
Heavy legs after travel or multiple games
Local soreness, swelling, or an active injury
Warm-up, activation, or range-of-motion work
2

Use The Lowest-Cost Effective Move

Sleep, meal, water, walk, or easy mobility first.
Add one targeted tool only if it fits the need.
Escalate pain, swelling, or injury questions to medical staff.
3

Check The Result

What changed after using the tool?

Is it worth repeating?

A recovery tool should solve a problem, not become a ritual you never question.

Body Tools Guide08
Athlete Safety

Protect Your Health And Career

You Are Responsible For What Enters Your Body.

These are my personal experiences, not medical advice. Before adding a supplement or recovery modality, speak with a qualified physician, sports dietitian, athletic trainer, or your team’s performance staff.

Check the current prohibited list and the rules that apply to your league.
Use independent sport-testing directories such as NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport.
Verify the exact product and, when available, the exact batch or lot number.
Keep packaging, receipts, lot numbers, and a written record of what you take.
Review possible interactions, side effects, doses, and timing with qualified staff.
Remember that third-party testing can reduce risk, but it cannot make supplement use risk-free.
WADA does not approve or certify supplements.

Anti-doping rules apply strict liability: the athlete is responsible for prohibited substances found in their body. Protect your availability, reputation, and career by treating every supplement decision seriously.
